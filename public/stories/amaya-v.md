> Many landlords in Milwaukee do not accept energy assistance or discriminate against tenants who need it.

My name is Amaya. I am a 24-year-old Native American/Latina woman who has lived in Milwaukee since 2016. For the first 3 years in the city, I lived in university housing as I was attending UWM studying social work. However, during the summer of 2019, I finally moved into my own place with my sibling and their partner.

Affording housing and utilities was difficult, even between the three of us, and often a burden as a full-time student whose schedule only allowed them to work part-time. Further, the property manager (Dominion Properties) with whom we rented from did not accept energy assistance payments from tenants. This resulted in us relying on loan repayment checks from school to afford energy costs but fortunately, we never missed a bill during this time thanks to that money. Bill payment time was always very stressful for us and it would throw off my mood and focus each month. It caused my parents to become distressed as well out of fear that my sibling and I would be late on a payment or be unable to afford the bill altogether.

This burden would continue even after I graduated and got my first "adult" job in Social Work. Many landlords in Milwaukee do not accept energy assistance or discriminate against tenants who need it. Additionally, landlords do not update windows, doors, insulation, etc within their properties to reduce energy costs, resulting in tenants paying more for something that could have been prevented in the first place. Although the city cannot shut off heating for residents during the winter months, it was a daily "battle" to be able to keep warm enough in my home while also being mindful of the amount of energy we used and how it would affect our final bill.

Nobody should have to choose between being comfortable and safe within their home in order to save some money. I hope sharing my story will shed some light on the unique yet unifying experiences the residents of Milwaukee face as it pertains to energy costs. There are many individuals and families who did not have the "safety nets" I do and had to make very difficult decisions or worse.
